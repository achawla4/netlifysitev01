Humility 
By a humble Kriyavan

In ancient texts and philosophical thought, humility is considered a supreme virtue. However, based on its underlying source, there is a profound and clear distinction between "humility born of ignorance" and "humility born of wisdom." We can understand this as the difference between truth and pretense, or between weakness and inner strength.

Humility born of ignorance arises from fear, helplessness, or an inferiority complex. When a person lacks knowledge of their true nature, capabilities, or the ultimate truth, they become timid and cloak this weakness under the guise of humility. This type of humility is often expressed out of fear of a powerful person or to fulfill a selfish motive. It does not stem from the heart but is a forced reaction dictated by circumstances. As soon as such a person gains power, authority, or strength, this facade of humility quickly turns into arrogance. In this state, the ego does not die; it is merely suppressed by external pressures. Ultimately, this is not true humility, but merely an expression of cowardice and ignorance.

On the other hand, ancient scriptures state "Vidya Dadati Vinayam," which means true knowledge bestows humility. Humility born of wisdom emerges naturally from self-realization and a deep understanding of the truth. As a person's knowledge expands, they realize their own smallness in the face of the infinite universe and the vast ocean of wisdom. This profound realization dissolves their ego. A truly wise person sees the exact same divine consciousness in every living being, and therefore, they naturally hold deep respect and compassion for everyone. This humility comes not from fear, but from absolute self-confidence and inner peace. It is not a trait of the weak, but rather the greatest ornament of those who are spiritually and mentally strong. Just as a tree laden with fruit naturally bends towards the earth, a heart filled with profound knowledge naturally becomes gentle and grounded.

This difference can be understood through a simple example. If someone shows humility out of fear of the law or punishment, it is humility born of ignorance. However, if someone is humble because they recognize that the same consciousness resides in the other person as in themselves, it is humility born of wisdom. This distinction is also evident in the pursuit of learning—one might bow down out of intimidation by complex words, while another bows down in pure gratitude after experiencing the depth of their meaning. In conclusion, humility born of ignorance is a kind of darkness and bondage, whereas humility born of wisdom is a true sign of inner freedom and light. For any seeker or student, humility born of wisdom is the fertile ground where the true seeds of education and enlightenment can sprout and flourish.
