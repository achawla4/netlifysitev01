Prajnanabha / Volume 1 Issue 9 / Editor's Message

PRAJNANABHA: Information and Physics (Volume 1, Issue 9 — August 2026)

REAL Institute, Gurugram, India

Dear Inquisitive Reader,

You hold in your hands or on-screen Issue 9 (August 2026) of the journal of the REAL Institute. The issue contains a selection of articles on a diverse range of sub-topics under the general heading, "Information and Physics." This heading is broad enough to include artificial intelligence and any other field where bits or signals come into play. And of course, physics is a broad topic in and of itself, including sub-fields like biophysics as well.

I take this opportunity to reflect on the journey of Prajnanabha, beginning with the first issue in December last. Almost every issue has had diverse sub-topic ranges included. However, some issues have been deeply mathematical, especially the last couple of ones, whereas others have had a relatively lower dose of deep math. This tells us that though information and physics both rely on mathematics, they are not math in and of themselves. Math gives them rigor and expository discipline, though the underlying base is conceptual or at the level of paradigms and modes of thinking.

And here we are thinking about the natural world, the man-made ecosystem, and the universe in general. We are pondering over their features, looking for ways to comprehend them in a pithy and effective or efficient manner. This compressed understanding serves man well.

There's a fable about a fox and a cat who were faced with an approaching lion. The cat simply ran up the tree — it was the only route it knew — whereas the so-called smart fox was eaten up as it was unsure which of its repertoire of routes to employ in the present setting. This is not to diminish the value of exploratory research, but at the same time, one must cull to the essential concepts. In this sense, the present issue does present some essential concepts.

Some of the reader classes who might benefit from this issue include:

  • Theoretical physicists
  • AI researchers
  • Philosophers of science
  • Space mission designers
  • Ethicists and policymakers

Wishing you joyful reading,

Br. Aman Chawla (Giri)
REAL Institute, Gurugram, India
30 July 2026
