ABSTRACT

We develop a space-frequency-domain formulation of the electromagnetic rim field generated by ion channels at nodes of Ranvier. The electromagnetic field is represented through the evanescent sector of the electromagnetic Green propagator rather than through a purely quasistatic dipole approximation. The resulting rim-zone field is derived explicitly in mixed Fourier representation and compared against the ordinary quasistatic dipole limit. Quantization of the evanescent sector is then carried out and compared structurally with Keller’s near-field quantum electrodynamics. The formulation does not assume biologically protected coherence. Instead, quantization is introduced as a mathematically natural extension of the evanescent electromagnetic sector. Explicit order-of-magnitude estimates are developed for field strengths, interaction energies, induced membrane voltages, and decoherence scales.

1 Introduction

Nodes of Ranvier contain dense concentrations of sodium and potassium ion channels responsible for saltatory conduction. During action potential propagation, ionic transport across the membrane generates localized electromagnetic fields capable of influencing neighboring axons. Conventional treatments model these interactions using:

1. quasistatic extracellular potentials,

2. cable-theoretic ephaptic coupling,

3. volume-conductor theory,

4. finite-element field simulations.

The present work does not replace these approaches. Instead, it reformulates the microscopic electromagnetic structure of nodal interaction using near-field electrodynamics in the spacefrequency domain. The central claim is not that biological tissue supports freely propagating electromagnetic radiation at neural frequencies. Rather, the relevant electromagnetic sector is overwhelmingly evanescent and matter-attached. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section 2 introduces the space-frequencydomain formulation of Maxwell’s equations, while Section 3 derives the scalar rim propagator and Section 4 extends this to the dyadic Green propagator. Section 5 addresses finite-size ion channel clusters, and Section 6 compares the near-zone electric field components with the quasistatic limit. Biophysical estimates are provided in Section 7, followed by the quantization of the rim field in Section 8. Section 9 draws structural parallels with Keller’s near-field QED, and Section 10 discusses the theoretical and experimental implications, including falsifiability and future directions. Appendices A–F provide supplementary derivations, estimates, and comparisons.

11 Discussion and Experimental Outlook

The present work should be interpreted primarily as a theoretical proposal concerning the microscopic electromagnetic structure of nodal interaction rather than as a completed predictive theory of neural communication. The central result is the identification of the biologically relevant electromagnetic sector as overwhelmingly evanescent and matter-attached. In the low-frequency biological regime:

kr ≪1, (53)

the ordinary quasistatic dipole field emerges naturally from the underlying rim propagator. Consequently, the present framework remains compatible with established quasistatic ephaptic and volume-conductor models while refining their spectral and propagator-theoretic interpretation. Importantly, the present work does not claim:

1. long-range coherent quantum communication in neural tissue,

2. biologically protected quantum states,

3. macroscopic quantum computation in the brain,

4. experimentally established photon-embryo dynamics.

Instead, the quantized treatment is introduced as a mathematically natural extension of the evanescent electromagnetic sector. At physiological temperature: T ∼300 K, (54)

environmental decoherence is expected to be rapid due to:

1. ionic collisions,

2. solvent interactions,

3. dielectric losses,

4. membrane fluctuations.

Thus the quantized rim modes should primarily be interpreted as localized field-theoretic excitations associated with near-field electrodynamics rather than as long-lived coherent biological states. Nevertheless, the formalism suggests several potentially testable biophysical consequences.

11.1 Predicted Scaling Behavior

The dominant near-zone interaction obeys:

Enear ∝1

r3 . (55)

Consequently:

1. tightly packed axons should exhibit stronger microscopic coupling,

2. demyelinated geometries should enhance interaction strength,

3. nodal synchronization effects should depend strongly on inter-axonal spacing.

Because: q ≫k (56)

throughout the biologically relevant regime, propagating electromagnetic radiation is expected to remain negligible compared with localized evanescent coupling.

11.2 Possible Experimental Signatures

The framework suggests several experimentally accessible signatures:

1. enhanced ephaptic synchronization in densely packed fiber tracts,

2. geometry-dependent conduction-velocity perturbations,

3. localized extracellular near-field gradients near nodes of Ranvier,

4. increased interaction strength under partial demyelination,

5. separation-dependent modulation of induced membrane voltages.

Potential experimental approaches include:

1. nanoelectrode measurements near individual nodes,

2. high-resolution extracellular field mapping,

3. controlled axon-separation experiments,

4. finite-element simulations combined with spectral decomposition,

5. correlation of tract geometry with synchronization behavior.

11.3 Relation to Conventional Models

The present formulation does not invalidate conventional quasistatic or volume-conductor theory. Instead, it provides a more explicit characterization of the dominant spectral sector underlying those models. In conventional coordinate-space treatments, the near field appears as:

Enear ∝1

r3 . (57)

In the present formulation, this behavior emerges from the evanescent spectral propagator:

˜$grim = 1$

2κe−κ|z|. (58)

Thus the quasistatic near field may be interpreted as the coordinate-space manifestation of an underlying evanescent electrodynamic sector.

11.4 Falsifiability

The present proposal would lose motivation if:

1. all microscopic nodal interactions are fully reproduced by purely resistive quasistatic models without requiring evanescent spectral localization,

2. measured extracellular near-node fields fail to exhibit the predicted near-zone scaling behavior,

3. geometry-dependent coupling effects remain absent even in tightly packed or demyelinated configurations.

Accordingly, the proposal is experimentally falsifiable in principle.

11.5 Outlook

The present work suggests several directions for future investigation:

1. finite-element implementation of the rim propagator,

2. incorporation of dielectric dispersion and anisotropic tissue conductivity,

3. coupling between multiple neighboring nodes,

4. stochastic channel-noise effects,

5. comparison with experimentally measured extracellular field distributions,

6. covariant near-field formulations in biological media.

More broadly, the work suggests that microscopic neural electrodynamics may profitably be viewed through the framework of near-field electrodynamics rather than solely through macroscopic circuit analogies.

12 Conclusion

We developed a detailed space-frequency-domain formulation of the electromagnetic rim field generated by ion channels at nodes of Ranvier. Explicit expressions for the dyadic rim propagator and dominant near-zone field components were derived. The formulation reproduces the conventional quasistatic dipole limit while simultaneously identifying the underlying spectral sector as overwhelmingly evanescent. The resulting framework refines the microscopic electrodynamic interpretation of ephaptic and extracellular interaction by explicitly characterizing the dominant biological electromagnetic sector as localized, longitudinally structured, and matter-attached.

B Appendix B: Decoherence and Classicality

The present formulation does not require long-lived biological quantum coherence. At: T ∼300 K, (68)

environmental decoherence is expected to occur rapidly through:

1. ionic collisions,

2. solvent interactions,

3. membrane fluctuations,

4. dielectric losses.

The characteristic ion-channel activation timescale is:

τchannel ∼10−4 −10−3 s. (69)

Electromagnetic decoherence times are expected to be substantially shorter. Consequently, the quantized rim field should primarily be interpreted as:

1. a mathematically consistent quantization of the evanescent sector,

2. a propagator-theoretic refinement of near-field electrodynamics,

3. a microscopic interpretation of localized field-matter coupling.

The formalism therefore remains compatible with effectively classical biological behavior.
