As per the Heine-Borel theorem [1] every closed and bounded subset of the reals is compact. Consider the spatio-temporal region occupied by the Earth. Clearly, it is closed and bounded as a subset of the universal spacetime manifold. Thus every covering of it will have a nite subcover that also covers it. In relation to this, consider a blackhole, or the big bang itself. These regions contain singularities which we assume may not be compact topologically. Thus the above conclusion might not apply to the big bang or to black holes. Suppose it doesn't apply. This leaves the door open for the following speculation.
