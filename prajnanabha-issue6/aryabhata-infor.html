Abstract

This monograph develops a unified theory of information based on a conservation principle that applies across classical, quantum, and interacting systems.

We introduce a general class of admissible information systems and derive a unique information functional that recovers both Shannon entropy and von Neumann entropy as special cases.

The central result, the Aryabhata Conservation Theorem, establishes that information is conserved under closed transformations and changes under open or coupled transformations via structured boundary terms.

These boundary terms admit a decomposition into components corresponding to interaction, coarse-graining, and finite-scale effects. In particular, the atypical sector of quantum systems is shown to govern deviations from idealized asymptotic behavior.

The framework unifies classical and quantum information theory while extending them to systems where independence assumptions fail. It also provides a structural explanation for the breakdown of source–channel separation and introduces a new perspective on entropy as a conserved quantity with boundary corrections.

Preface

The concept of information lies at the foundation of modern science. It governs communication systems, quantum mechanics, statistical physics, and increasingly, biological and cognitive processes.

Yet, despite its central role, information remains fragmented across disciplines. Classical information theory, quantum information theory, and physical models of information each provide powerful but incomplete perspectives.

This work arose from the belief that information should admit a unified description—one that treats it as a fundamental quantity governed by general principles analogous to conservation laws in physics.

The Aryabhata Conservation Theorem presented in this monograph is an attempt to formalize this idea.

The development proceeds from classical entropy and quantum source coding to a general framework of admissible systems, culminating in a conservation law with structured boundary terms.

A key insight is that deviations from idealized behavior—particularly in finite or coupled systems—are not merely errors but carry internal structure. The atypical sector, traditionally discarded in asymptotic analysis, plays a central role in this formulation.

This work is intended as a starting point rather than a final statement. Many aspects, particularly in continuous and highly interacting systems, require further development.

Nevertheless, it is hoped that the framework presented here provides a useful step toward a unified theory of information.

The Problem of Information Universality

Introduction

Modern science employs multiple, seemingly incompatible notions of information. In classical communication theory, information is quantified through entropy and coding rates. In quantum theory, it appears through density operators, von Neumann entropy, and channel capacities. In neuroscience and physical systems, information is often treated as flow, structure, or constraint.

Despite their success within their respective domains, these notions lack a single, system-independent formulation. There is no universally accepted quantity that:

  • applies equally to classical, quantum, and physical systems,

  • behaves consistently under transformation,

  • admits a conservation law analogous to energy or momentum.

This monograph addresses this gap.

The central claim developed throughout this work is that:

Information is a conserved quantity across admissible systems, and classical and quantum information measures arise as special cases of a more general invariant.

The purpose of this chapter is to formalize the problem that necessitates such a theorem.

In classical information theory, a discrete memoryless source $X$ with distribution $p(x)$ is characterized by its entropy \[ H(X) = - _x p(x) p(x). \]

  • probabilistic,

  • message-centric,

  • asymptotic.

Information in Physical and Cognitive Systems

Beyond communication theory, information appears in broader contexts:

  • neural systems (e.g., ephaptic coupling),

  • physical fields and spacetime,

In these settings, information is not naturally expressed as a probability distribution over messages. Instead, it appears as:

  • transformation,

The Central Gap

The preceding discussion reveals a fundamental tension.

  • Classical information is defined on probability distributions.

  • Quantum information is defined on operators.

  • Physical information appears as flow or geometry.

There is no single object ${I}$ such that:

  • ${I}$ reduces to $H(X)$ in the classical case,

  • ${I}$ reduces to $S()$ in the quantum case,

  • ${I}$ remains meaningful for interacting or continuous systems.

Moreover, no general conservation law is known that applies across these domains.

This motivates the central question:

Does there exist a universal notion of information that is invariant under admissible system evolution?

A Guiding Observation from Quantum Source Coding

Quantum source coding provides a crucial clue.

The spectral decomposition of a density operator \[ = _i _i |e_i e_i| \] induces a classical probability distribution ${_i}$.

For blocklength $n$, the eigenvalues of $^{ n}$ take the form \[ _{i_1} _{i_n}, \] which coincide exactly with the probabilities of an i.i.d.\ classical source :contentReference[oaicite:4]{index=4}.

This implies:

Quantum information theory is structurally governed by a hidden classical probability law.

However, this reduction comes at a cost.

The standard theory:

  • retains only the typical subspace,

  • discards the atypical sector as negligible.

Yet the atypical sector is not structureless. It is a low-weight spectral tail with internal organization at finite blocklength :contentReference[oaicite:5]{index=5}.

This observation suggests that:

Information may be governed by a deeper structure than entropy alone.

Toward a Conservation Principle

In parallel, a separate line of work proposes that:

Information is conserved across all closed systems, including quantum and observer-dependent processes :contentReference[oaicite:6]{index=6}.

This proposal elevates information from a descriptive quantity to a dynamical invariant, analogous to energy.

However, this principle is currently:

  • conceptually stated,

  • not yet embedded in a unified mathematical framework,

  • not connected rigorously to coding theorems.

The Aryabhata Program

This monograph develops a unified framework resolving the above tensions.

The program proceeds as follows:

  1. Define a class of admissible information systems.

  2. Construct a generalized information functional ${I}$.

  3. Establish its transformation properties.

  4. Prove a conservation law under admissible dynamics.

  5. Recover classical and quantum information as special cases.

Preview of the Main Theorem

The entire development culminates in a single result.

(Informal Statement) There exists a functional ${I}$ defined on admissible information-bearing systems such that, under admissible transformations $T$, \[ {I}(T(x)) = {I}(x) + {B}(x,T), \] where ${B}$ is a boundary term vanishing for closed systems.

Furthermore:

  • ${I}$ reduces to Shannon entropy in the classical case,

  • ${I}$ reduces to von Neumann entropy in the quantum case,

  • deviations from classical behavior arise through nontrivial boundary terms.

The Aryabhata Conservation Theorem

The theorem establishes that information behaves as a conserved quantity with structured deviations.
