Quantum Evolution and Clustering Probabilities in a Vortex Fluid: Analogy with a BEC

Chawla, Aman

Abstract

—This note analyzes the unitary quantum dynamics of an N-vortex fluid governed by a pairwise logarithmic Hamilto- nian. Starting from either a multi-branch cat state or a squeezed cluster state, we derive the probability that all vortices remain localized within a distance δ of a single vortex site after unitary evolution. We then apply a coordinate reflection operator and show that this probability remains unchanged due to the system’s symmetry. I. INTRODUCTION Point vortices in two-dimensional ideal fluids exemplify Hamiltonian many-body interactions and chaos [3]. Their quantum version features nontrivial spreading and entangle- ment and an analogy with a BEC with N atoms can be drawn. We study the quantum Hamiltonian for N point vortices with circulations Γα and complex positions zα = xα + iyα: H = −1 4π X α<β ΓαΓβ log |zα −zβ|. Classically, the coordinates are cross-coupled: ˙xα = 1 Γα ∂H ∂yα , ˙yα = −1 Γα ∂H ∂xα . II. QUANTUM INITIAL STATES AND EVOLUTION Quantization promotes {xα, yα} to operators with canonical commutators. The unitary evolution is |ψ(T)⟩= e−iHT/ℏ|ψ(0)⟩. We consider two initial states: • Cat state: an equal superposition of clusters with all vortices coinciding at each of the N sites. • Squeezed state: a single branch with relative coordinates squeezed along a chosen quadrature. III. CLUSTER PROBABILITY We define the barycenter and relative coordinates: ZB = 1 N X α zα, ξj = zj −ZB. A measurement checks if all ξj satisfy |ξj| < δ. Due to the log repulsion, the relative coordinates spread with time: P(δ, T) ≈ δ4 [σ2 0 + V 2T 2]2 , (N = 3), where σ0 is the initial cluster width and V characterizes the spreading rate. IV. REFLECTION OPERATOR We define a reflection operator: R : zα 7→2z(0) α −zα. This maps ξj 7→−ξj but keeps ZB unchanged. Since the Hamiltonian depends only on pairwise distances, it is invariant under R. The measurement region |ξj| < δ is also invariant. Therefore, PR(δ, T) = P(δ, T) for both cat and squeezed states. V. CONCLUSION The probability that all vortices remain within a small cluster decays polynomially in time due to the logarithmic repulsion. This can be thought of as the reverse of BEC formation. Applying a reflection operator has no effect on this probability due to the symmetry of the Hamiltonian and measurement condition. For squeezed states, the spreading rate may differ, but the invariance argument holds.
