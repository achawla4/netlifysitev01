Article 1

Bi-stochastic Versions of Hairer Coupling

A. Chawla
REAL Institute, Gurugram
December 27, 2025

Abstract

Coupling methods provide powerful tools for studying long-time behavior of stochastic dynamical systems. Hairer (2001) introduced an influential framework of asymptotic coupling to obtain exponential mixing results for stochastic partial differential equations. In his presentation, the bi-stochastic process arises as a pair of state and noise variables on a single probability space. In this note we propose a bi-stochastic formulation built on two distinct probability spaces, and show how the original Hairer construction is recovered as a special case. We also discuss how asymptotic coupling arguments adapt to this broader framework.

Article 3

Synaptic and Ephaptic Proposals for Selective Attention: A Comparative Study

A. Chawla
IIT Delhi and REAL Institute
aman.chawla@gmail.com

Abstract

We compare two mathematical frameworks for understanding selective attention in neural systems: the synaptic-based Free Energy Principle of Friston, which posits that brains minimize prediction error through hierarchical Bayesian inference, and the ephaptic coupling framework of Chawla and Morgera, which demonstrates that electromagnetic field interactions between adjacent axons automatically amplify high-entropy signals. These approaches offer fundamentally different explanations for how nervous systems select salient stimuli. Friston's framework requires complex computational cascades involving precision weighting, hierarchical revision, and widespread cortical recruitment. In contrast, ephaptic coupling provides a physical substrate whereby high-information-content signals automatically dominate through local electromagnetic interactions in axon tracts. We argue that ephaptic coupling may provide the physical mechanism underlying selective attention, operating at the substrate level rather than as an algorithmic process.

Article 4

White Matter Capacity

Aman Chawla
REAL Institute, Gurugram

Abstract

This paper develops an explicit capacity analysis of the axon tract modeled as a vector Gaussian communication channel with perfect feedback. Building on the generalized ephaptic equation and its modifications, we show how the tract can be reduced to an effective linear Gaussian channel with feedback eliminated. We then derive finite-block capacity expressions, discuss waterfilling and eigenmode allocation, and extend to the stationary infinite-horizon case. Illustrative examples highlight the role of ephaptic coupling in modifying channel capacity. The work bridges neuroscience and information theory, offering a rigorous framework for quantifying the fundamental limits of communication in axon tracts.

The Action Potential Field: Why Consciousness May be Irreducible

A. Chawla
REAL Institute and IIT Delhi
December 25, 2025

Abstract

This work develops a field-theoretic framework for understanding neural dynamics and consciousness. We introduce the action potential (AP) field A(r,t) as a fundamental continuous entity from which localized neural phenomena emerge. The framework demonstrates that conscious experience may correspond to irreducible global field configurations that resist decomposition into independent components. Through detailed analysis of synchronization phenomenology, EEG measurements, Independent Component Analysis, and the emergence of perceptual illusions, we show that many neural phenomena are better understood as properties of continuous excitable fields rather than as discrete neuronal computations. The work includes derivations of exact onset times for conscious perception, field-theoretic reformulations of Integrated Information Theory, and extensions to curved spacetime geometries that account for anatomical structure.

Article 6

Minimal Systems for Computational Creativity: Evidence of Motif Stabilization

A. Chawla
REAL Institute and IIT Delhi

Abstract

This paper presents and analyzes a series of Python-based generative systems designed to explore computational creativity through explicit generative spaces, rule-based perceptual evaluation, and iterative refinement. The systems, developed sequentially as godtron, davincitron, and vincination, generate visual artifacts composed of simple geometric primitives under progressively tighter evaluative constraints. Unlike data-driven generative models, the approach emphasizes transparency, inspectability, and minimalism. We describe the system architecture, execution model, and predicted behavior, and substantiate claims of motif stabilization using representative visual artifacts produced by each system stage. The results support the claim that even weak evaluative mechanisms, when applied iteratively, can induce structural regularity and emergent motifs.

Article 7

Quantifying the Quantum Intelligence of a Language

A. Chawla
REAL Institute and IIT Delhi

Abstract

This work proposes a novel framework for quantifying the "intelligence" of a language by drawing analogies between formal grammars and probability spaces. We interpret Chomskian grammars through the lens of communication theory, treating language transformation (such as Sanskrit corrupting to Hindi) as a stochastic channel. The "intelligence" of a receiving language is defined as the minimum maximum of the reliability-rate function E(R) over all transmission-reception schemes and source languages. This framework is extended to "quantum intelligence" by allowing quantum mechanical transmission-reception schemes that incorporate noncommutativity of observables. The approach provides a rigorous information-theoretic measure of linguistic decoding efficiency and can be empirically tested through carefully constructed grammar experiments.
