Quantifying the Impact of Lift-off on the Optic Nerve Tract of Space Crew

Chawla, Aman

Abstract

In this note we demonstrate the impact of severe vibrations in a pas- senger vehicle upon a nerve tract's conduction of action potentials, under the ephaptic geometric setting rst considered in 2016-2017. As an ap- plication, this work could be used to determine how nerve signals of crew optic tracts might be scrambled during the various phases of space travel, from the point of view of space vehicle vibrations. The lift-o phase of launching a rocket has a high vibrational impact on the rocket's structure which in turn must have some impact on the crew vehicle and the crew (for example, [1]). In order to quantify the signicance of this impact for neural information processing, we modied the MATLAB program developed in [2, 3] by allowing the inter-axonal distance matrix (denoted in [2] as Wij) to be perturbed by noise in each entry, even as time evolved. When the added noise variance in each entry was 0.01 in magnitude, we obtained baseline- like propagation (i.e., similar to what is obtained in the absence of noise). This is shown in Figure 1. When the added noise variance was augmented by another additive term of 0.01 magnitude, so that the total variance was 0.02, double the previous value, the prole of propagation was signicantly altered. Please see Figure 2. The action potential initiation time on axon 3 was severely delayed as compared to baseline, so that the third axon red after the second axon, in- stead of before it. This demonstrates a clear impact on time-coded information processing due to an axon tract being immersed in a eld of mechanical vibra- tions. In the case of an optic nerve carrying visual information to the brain, we can surmise that these signals would now carry altered informational content to subsequent processing centers.
