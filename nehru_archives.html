REAL Nehru Archives is an independent Seva initiative of the Hariharananda REAL Institute dedicated to cataloging, preserving, and providing digital access to historical manuscripts, speeches, personal papers, and published works of Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964), India's first Prime Minister, along with materials from the Indian Freedom Movement.

REAL Nehru Archives

Preserving the Historical Manuscripts, Speeches & Works of Jawaharlal Nehru

Offered in the spirit of disinterested service (Seva), this archive documents the literary, philosophical, epistolary, and diplomatic corpus of Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964).

“Culture is the widening of the mind and of the spirit. It is never a narrowing of the mind or a restriction of the human spirit or of the country's spirit.” — Jawaharlal Nehru

Archival Purpose & Scope

Jawaharlal Nehru (1889–1964) left behind a vast corpus of historical writings, prison journals, epistolary correspondences, statecraft papers, and philosophical commentaries that chronicle the Indian freedom movement and the founding era of modern statecraft.

This digital repository catalogs key collections including the Selected Works of Jawaharlal Nehru (SWJN), his major prison monographs (The Discovery of India, Glimpses of World History, An Autobiography), and foundational public speeches.

As part of REAL's heritage preservation initiatives, this page serves as an open scholarly reference index to these manuscripts, letters, and published works for researchers, historians, and seekers worldwide.

Four Pillars of the Nehru Corpus

📚

Selected Works of Jawaharlal Nehru (SWJN)

Over 100 volumes compiled by the Jawaharlal Nehru Memorial Fund, dividing Nehru's writings, letters, and official notes into First Series (1903–1947) and Second Series (1947–1964).

✍️

Major Literary Monographs

Classics written during imprisonment: The Discovery of India (Ahmadnagar Fort), Glimpses of World History (Letters to Indira), and Toward Freedom: An Autobiography.

🎙️

Historic Speeches & Broadcasts

Orations that defined modern statecraft: "Tryst with Destiny" (1947), "The Light Has Gone Out" (1948), Asian Relations Conference (1947), and Non-Alignment summit addresses.

✉️

Epistolary & Private Papers

Correspondences with Mahatma Gandhi, Rabindranath Tagore, Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose, Sardar Patel, Albert Einstein, Bertrand Russell, and international state heads.

Speech 14 Aug 1947

Tryst with Destiny

Constituent Assembly of India, New Delhi

“Long years ago we made a tryst with destiny, and now the time comes when we shall redeem our pledge... At the stroke of the midnight hour, when the world sleeps, India will awake to life and freedom.”

Monograph 1942 – 1946

The Discovery of India

Written at Ahmadnagar Fort Prison (595 pp.)

“India is a geographical and economic entity, a cultural unity amidst diversity, a bundle of contradictions held together by strong but invisible threads.”

Speech 30 Jan 1948

The Light Has Gone Out

All India Radio Address on Gandhi's Passing

“The light has gone out of our lives and there is darkness everywhere... Our beloved leader, Bapu as we called him, the Father of the Nation, is no more.”

Monograph 1930 – 1933

Glimpses of World History

196 Letters Written to Indira Gandhi from Prison

“If we are to understand the present, we must know something of the past... History is not just a sequence of kings and wars, but the unfolding of human mind and civilization.”

Letter Series Summer 1928

Letters from a Father to His Daughter

31 Letters on Earth's Formation & Early Civilizations

“You are in Mussoorie and I am in Allahabad... When you and I are together you often ask me questions about many things. I am sending you letters about the story of our earth.”

Statecraft 29 Apr 1954

Panchsheel & Non-Alignment (NAM)

Sino-Indian Agreement & Bandung Conference 1955

“Mutual respect for territorial integrity, non-aggression, non-interference in internal affairs, equality and mutual benefit, and peaceful coexistence.”

Correspondence 1947 – 1953

Exchange with Albert Einstein

Letters on Nuclear Disarmament & World Peace

“Science has given man immense power over nature, but unless guided by moral wisdom, that power risks self-destruction. India advocates disarmament and peaceful technology.”

Speech 23 Mar 1947

Inaugural Address, Asian Relations Conference

Purana Qila, New Delhi

“Asia is finding herself again... For too long we of Asia have been petitioners in western courts and chanceries. That story must belong to the past.”

Autobiography 1934 – 1935

Toward Freedom: An Autobiography

Written in Dehradun & Almora Jails

“I have become a queer mixture of the East and the West, out of place everywhere, at home nowhere. Perhaps my thoughts and approach to life are more akin to what is called Western than Eastern.”
