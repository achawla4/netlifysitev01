Vedic Approach to a Short Internode

Chawla, Aman

Abstract

In this brief paper, the authors present the Vedic solution of the dis- cretized equation representing the internode of a myelinated axon. The Vedic method for solving inhomogeneous simultaneous equations is adopted in this paper. The method makes use of the formula samkalana-vyavakalanabhyam presented in [1]. The equation to be solved is, C ∂V (x, t) ∂t = κ∂2V (x, t) ∂x2 −GV (x, t). (1) We make use of the discretization prescription, ∂V (x, t) ∂t = V (i, n + 1) −V (i, n) △t , (2) ∂2V (x, t) ∂x2 = 2(△x)2 [(V (i + 1, n + 1) −2V (i, n + 1) + V (i −1, n + 1)) + ...] , (3) ... + (V (i + 1, n) −2V (i, n) + V (i −1, n))] (4) and V (x, t) = V (i, n + 1) + V (i, n) . (5) Utilizing the discretization, we obtain, αV (i, n+1)+βV (i, n)−γ [V (i + 1, n + 1) + V (i −1, n + 1) + V (i + 1, n) + V (i −1, n)] = 0. (6) Suppose, i is 1 or 2. That is, we have a two segment axon. This yields the following simultaneous equations where the i = 1 case is displayed rst, and then the i = 2 case: αV (1, n + 1) + βV (1, n) −γ [V (2, n + 1) + V (0, n + 1) + V (2, n) + V (0, n)] = 0 (7) 1

and αV (2, n + 1) + βV (2, n) −γ [V (3, n + 1) + V (1, n + 1) + V (3, n) + V (1, n)] = 0 (8) Next, we denote, V (0, n) as x, V (0, n + 1) as y, V (1, n) as z, V (1, n + 1) as a, V (2, n) as b and V (2, n + 1) as c, V (3, n) as d and V (3, n + 1) as e for this truncated problem. We will assume that V (·, n) is the known case and we will nd the solution for the n + 1 case. The base case, V (·, 1) is the initial condition, which is also assumed to be given. Thus induction will complete the entire temporal trajectory. The boundary conditions determine V (0, ·) and V (3, ·). In this case, those are x, y, d and e. The two simultaneous equations become, αa −γc = −βz + γy + γb + γx (9) and −γa + αc = −βb + γd + γe + γz. (10) As per the sutra, we nd their sum and dierence to ease out the solution. Thus, (α −γ)a + (α −γ)c = −β(z + b) + γ(y + b + x + d + e + z) (11) and (α + γ)a −(α + γ)c = β(b −z) + γ(y + b + x −d −e −z). (12) Upon simplication we get, (α −γ)(a + c) = RHS1 (13) and (α + γ)(a −c) = RHS2 (14) These are easily used to get, a = RHS1 2(α −γ) + RHS2 2(α + γ) (15) and hence, c = RHS1 2(α −γ) −RHS2 (α + γ). (16) With a and c, we obtain V (1, 2) and V (2, 2). Using these we would obtain V (1, 3) and V (2, 3) and so on, the entire temporal trajectory for the two seg- ments is determined. 2
