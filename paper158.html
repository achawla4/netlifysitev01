Towards a Vedic Algorithm for the Singular Value Decomposition: Part I - On Matrix Eigenvalues

Chawla, Aman

Abstract

In this note the authors outline a path towards a Vedic approach to the singular value decomposition which is important in quantum computing. This work is dedicated to the progenitor of Vedic math. Jagatguru Bharti Krishna Tirthaji introduced several techniques in Vedic math- ematics. He mentioned that some volumes of his work were lost. In this note we attempt to extend his available techniques to nding the singular value decom- position of a matrix. We use a Vedic approach to construct the Singular Value Decomposition (SVD) by performing a breakdown of linear algebra concepts like eigenvalues, the Spectral Theorem, and the Polar Decomposition. This work will focus on the rst concept, eigenvalues. We begin by outlining how the linear transformation (A −λI) can be used to nd the eigenvalues of A. Let's break this down step by step, leading up to nding the determinant and setting it to zero to solve for the eigenvalues using the Vedic method. Setting the Determinant to Zero More specically, to nd the eigenvalues, we set the determinant to zero to get an equation. Then we nd the roots of the equation in the Vedic approach [2]. The eigenvalues of a matrix (or transformation) play a crucial role in the SVD. By nding the eigenvalues of the matrix AT A, we can determine the singular values of the matrix A. Conclusion By leveraging the Vedic method in the derivation of eigenvalues, one can intu- itively build the SVD of a matrix. The step-by-step nature of Vedic mathematics 2
